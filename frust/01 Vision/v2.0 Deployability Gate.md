# The v2.0 Deployability Gate — Milestone 3 Capstone

> v1.0 asked *"is this a Frappe replacement?"* and answered it with evidence.
> v2.0 asks **"is it deployable, and where isn't it?"** — and its defining
> criterion is sharper than a re-score: **no bounded-by-assumption left.**

**Verdict: the gate PASSES — on the second pass.**

On 2026-07-28 the classification found **three** bounds held up by nothing but
the absence of a test, and the gate **blocked**. Three measurement WOs later
(WO-035, WO-036) the bucket is empty and every surviving bound has a number or a
named trade behind it.

**The first-pass failure is kept in this document deliberately.** Milestone 3
exists *because* v1.0 shipped concurrency "bounded" on an assumption nobody had
tested; a capstone that rubber-stamped three more would have repeated exactly
that. The gate's value is that it blocked — and the most instructive result is
A3, where the reasonable-sounding arithmetic (640 req/s) was wrong by **4.7×
and about which component was the ceiling.**

| # | the bound | what the walk actually found | resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~~**A1**~~ | ~~**P-2.2** — multi-app hook composition~~ | **CLOSED 2026-07-28 by [[2026-07-28 WO-036 multi-app composition and upgrade survival]].** Two answers, both measured: (a) a second app hooking a DocType it does not declare is **REFUSED at install, loudly** (`server_script targets 'shared_doc', which this bundle does not declare`) — apps are hook-isolated *by construction*, not by luck, and there is no silent last-writer-wins; (b) two apps installed together, each hooking its own DocType, run **their own hook with no cross-talk**. | ✅ **bounded-by-architecture** (one DocType = one owning app = one script) |
| ~~**A2**~~ | ~~**P-7.3** — major-upgrade survival~~ | **CLOSED 2026-07-28 by the same WO.** A real app (DocType + hook + rollup + data) installed, then the ADR-008 two-step upgrade driven (refused un-acked → applied acked). Post-upgrade the app's metadata, data and registry survive **and its hook still fires** — functional, not merely present. | ✅ **bounded-by-measurement** (upgrade proven; *longitudinal* multi-year history still cannot exist) |
| ~~**A3**~~ | ~~*(new — no P-row)* **Desk-tier concurrent request throughput**~~ | **CLOSED 2026-07-28 by [[2026-07-28 WO-035 desk concurrent load]].** Measured: **~135 req/s at 50 concurrent** (knee), bounded by the **DB, not the Desk** — the blocking-`ureq` structure does not bind at this scale. Contention answered: 48 SSE streams + 200 concurrent page requests, **neither starves the other** (0 failures, SSE keeps ticking). *The refused arithmetic predicted 640 req/s — wrong by 4.7× and for a different mechanism entirely.* | ✅ **bounded-by-measurement** |

> [!warning] NEW finding from closing A3 — **the Desk degrades by erroring, not by queueing**
> Past the ~50-concurrent knee the stack returns **HTTP 500** for a large share
> of requests (≈45% at 200, ≈85% at 500) rather than queueing — a load spike
> *fails* users instead of slowing them. Cause is DB saturation propagating up
> (`E_DB`), **not** the blocking-`ureq` structure. Related: realtime subscribe
> stays impaired for **>20 s** after heavy load (47/48 refused at +20 s; 48/48 on
> an unloaded stack). Wants admission control / backpressure — shed with intent
> (429/503) rather than 500 by accident. **→ its own WO; it does not re-open A3.**

**On A3 and the escalation clause:** it did **not** downgrade P-1.1. That verdict claims *the kernel no longer serializes*, which remains measured and true (124 req/s, DB-bound); A3 was a different tier. At the time of blocking, the arithmetic "suggested it was fine" at ~640 req/s — **the measurement came back at ~135, DB-bound.** Wrong in magnitude and in mechanism. That is the single best argument in this document for why "measure, don't infer" is a rule.

## The v2.0 verdict — deployable for what, not-yet for what

**Deployable for:** a **single-node, single-tenant-database** ERP workload with a
small-to-moderate user population. That claim rests on numbers, not confidence:

- **Concurrency** — 124 req/s, scaling with cores, **DB-bound not kernel-bound**
  (SurrealDB burns ~10× the kernel's CPU). The kernel is not the ceiling.
- **Memory** — **60 MB** resident idle, **84 MB** under 500 concurrent clients.
  One process, no per-worker multiplication.
- **Correctness** — money exact across storage, transport, kernel, rollup and
  script; permissions enforced by the database under the caller's own session;
  audit unbypassable because it is the changefeed.
- **Operability** — structured logs, tracing, `/metrics`; a *tested* DR runbook;
  a restore path secure by construction (the kernel refuses a compromised key).
- **Correctness under failure** — fail-closed boot, typed errors, no
  `ignore_permissions`, three silent failures converted to loud ones this
  milestone.

**Not yet deployable for:** multi-tenant SaaS needing **per-tenant restore**
(P-8.1 — the single sharpest architectural bound); deployments needing
**PITR** (snapshot-only, RPO = export interval); anything needing
**turnkey batteries** (print/PDF, email, report designer — the mail prototype is
in-tree but unbuilt into a feature); and anything needing **supervised
production packaging** (P-7.1 — `frust serve` is dev-run).

**Desk-tier capacity, now measured:** ~**135 req/s at 50 concurrent**, DB-bound,
with SSE streams and page traffic coexisting without starvation. **But past that
knee the Desk fails requests (HTTP 500) rather than queueing them** — a measured,
named limitation ([[WO-038 Desk Admission Control]]), not an assumption, so it
does not block this gate but does bound "deployable" to loads under the knee.

## Milestone status

**Milestone 3 is COMPLETE.** Its brief was "eliminate the assumptions v1.0
shipped with." Three were found at the capstone and all three are closed by
measurement — the bucket is empty.

**Sealed on a suite seen to finish: 271 passed · 0 failed · 6 ignored · 42
binaries** (the 6 are the perf/scale/restart gates that need their own
instances). Recorded here because the gate's rule applies to the gate itself: a
pass citing "the tests are green" without the number would be the same unearned
claim it spent two runs refusing. The milestone was briefly sealed on a suite
still in flight (120/0 across 26 of 42); that seal was withdrawn until this
number existed.

What Milestone 3 *did* deliver, all measured: concurrency 15 → 124 req/s (8.2×),
memory measured for the first time, two P0s that had shipped invisibly killed
(full-document hooks; the restore-path auth bypass), DR closed with a runbook
that tells the truth about no-PITR, the dependency current, money a clean sweep,
and the Desk's workflow overclaim corrected in a real browser.
